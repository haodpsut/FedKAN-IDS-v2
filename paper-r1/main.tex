\begin{abstract}
Kolmogorov--Arnold networks (KANs) have been proposed for federated intrusion detection on the
promise that, at a matched parameter budget, they are more accurate and more stable than
multilayer perceptrons under client heterogeneity. We test that promise on three standardised
NetFlow-v2 datasets and it does not survive a controlled comparison. The advantage is confounded
with how much tuning each architecture receives: sweeping the learning rate over six values, the
$+5.49$~pp advantage we ourselves reported on NF-BoT-IoT-v2 falls to $+0.76$~pp when the
parameter-matched MLP is given its own best rate, and to $-0.54$~pp ($p=0.58$) under a nested
protocol that selects the rate on ten seeds and reports on twenty never seen during selection.
Replacing FedAvg with FedProx is a second, independent correction of the same kind, and the two
compound: with both applied the MLP leads by $1.87$~pp. Across four cells the advantage ranges
from $-0.49$ to $+6.23$~pp with no consistent sign, and where clients are homogeneous the MLP is
clearly ahead. The companion variance-reduction claim reverses on three cells of four. At matched
parameters the KAN also costs $19.9\times$ more per-sample inference on CPU: parameter parity is
not cost parity. Our contribution is the controlled comparison and the protocol that makes it
controlled, released with $1{,}500$ runs.
\end{abstract}
